This summer, a group of Steve Hicks School of Social Work students, faculty and staff set out on a route that begins in Austin and ends in Belfast. Along the way they are asking a question that sits at the center of social work and reaches far beyond it: how do people who have stood on opposite sides of a conflict learn to talk to one another again?

The course is From Texas to the Troubles, the first signature program of the School's Office of Global Engagement (OGE). Launching in summer 2026, it takes participants from Austin to Dublin and on to Belfast, with a focus on constructive dialogue and leadership in divided societies.

## Why Northern Ireland

For three decades, the period known as the Troubles divided Northern Ireland and claimed more than 3,500 lives. The conflict formally ended with the 1998 Good Friday Agreement, and the peace that followed has been built, tested and maintained in the years since through sustained work at the community level.

That history makes Northern Ireland an unusually rich classroom for social work students. The region's experience of reconciliation offers lessons in how conflict and violence spread, how peacemaking and restorative justice take hold, and how leaders keep people talking when it would be easier to stop. Those lessons travel well, including to communities in Texas working through divisions of their own.

## Built on existing relationships

From Texas to the Troubles grows out of work the School was already doing in Northern Ireland. Through "Leading with Peace: Lessons from Northern Ireland," a President's Award for Global Learning program, Noël Busch-Armendariz, Bruce Kellison and Monica Martinez have led 15 students from many majors in partnership with Corrymeela, a community in Ballycastle whose mission is to build trust and understanding.

The new course also follows a precursor event held in Austin in late October 2025. "Constructive Dialogue, Poetry and Social Imagination" brought writers from the Seamus Heaney Centre at Queen's University Belfast to campus, with support from Robert Hull, Consul General of Ireland in Austin, and Jenny Browne, the 2018 Texas Poet Laureate.

Busch-Armendariz, the School's Associate Dean for Global Engagement, leads the Northern Ireland work. Her own international experience spans more than 40 years and includes Albania, Dubai, England, India, Ireland, Lebanon, Scotland and South Korea, as well as Peace Corps service in Romania.

> When a member of our social work community engages globally, we believe they leave to learn and return to lead.
>
> — Noël Busch-Armendariz, Associate Dean for Global Engagement

## Leave to learn, return to lead

That phrase has become OGE's student-facing tagline, and it captures the intent of the program. Participants are not traveling to observe a peace process from a distance. They are studying the practices of dialogue and leadership that made it possible, so they can bring those practices home to agencies, classrooms, clinics and communities across Texas.

OGE was created in 2023 to lead, support and coordinate the School's global engagement initiatives across three pillars: education abroad, research and service. From Texas to the Troubles is the office's first signature offering, and it reflects a broader aim of enhancing education that addresses global social issues while deepening partnerships with institutions worldwide.
