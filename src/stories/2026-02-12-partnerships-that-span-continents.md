In 1993, the Steve Hicks School of Social Work placed a student with the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees in Geneva. It was the School's first regular international placement, and more than three decades later the relationship is still going, now one of many that stretch from Santiago to Wellington and from Tallinn to Tamil Nadu.

Since then, students have completed placements on every continent except Antarctica, and faculty-led internships for MSSW students have run in 20 countries. "Global engagement has been a part of our signature education landscape for more than 20 years," Dean Allan Cole has said. What is new is the structure around it.

## A new office for a longstanding commitment

The Office of Global Engagement (OGE) was created in 2023 as a strategic priority aligned with the School's 2024–2026 priorities. Its charge is to lead, support and coordinate the School's global initiatives across three pillars: education abroad, research and service. Noël Busch-Armendariz serves as Associate Dean for Global Engagement, Tanya Voss as Assistant Dean and Caitlin Sulley as Director of Research Operations. <!-- VERIFY: The Utopian describes Sulley as "director of operations"; the site uses her directory title, Director of Research Operations. -->

"Global engagement represents one of the most strategic areas for our School," said Rubén Parra-Cardona, the office's founding Associate Dean, whose own research connects the School to Chile and Puerto Rico.

## Where students go

Each spring, final-year MSSW students can complete their advanced practicum abroad at sites OGE coordinates with the Office of Practicum Education and Texas Global:

- **Santiago, Chile:** a community-based hospital social work department and a program accompanying refugees and people in need of international protection.
- **Oaxaca, Mexico:** four agencies, from school-based outreach for children to an entrepreneurial program with Indigenous communities and a grassroots feminist organization.
- **Wellington, New Zealand:** counseling and wellness services, mainly for young adults ages 18 to 24.
- **Cape Town, South Africa:** clinical and policy placements in substance use treatment, housing advocacy, child welfare policy and child trauma counseling.
- **UNHCR in Geneva and Budapest:** program planning, monitoring and evaluation, and technical consulting on refugee responses in Ukraine, Syria, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Myanmar, Sudan and Haiti.

Undergraduates have their own pathway through Projects with Underserved Communities (PUC), the yearlong service-learning sequence run by Texas Global, the Cockrell School of Engineering and the School of Social Work. Since 2010, nearly 300 Longhorns have traveled to four continents through PUC, and this year's teams are working in Costa Rica, Greece and India. <!-- VERIFY: PUC launch year. ## Research across borders

The School's research partnerships are growing too. In Chile, faculty co-teach in the clinical master's program at Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile, the country's first, and collaborate with Fundación San Carlos de Maipo on a parenting intervention. In Puerto Rico, Cortando la Brecha builds research capacity among early- and mid-career scholars. In Estonia, Diana DiNitto has taught in Tallinn University's social work doctoral program since 2014, and a dissertation she co-supervised won a national award from the Estonian Research Council, the first for social work.

This spring, Estonian faculty are expected to make their first visit to Texas through the Erasmus+ exchange. <!-- VERIFY: visit dates and presenters (April 2026: Merike Sisask and Karmen Toros) -->

## What comes next

OGE's signature program, From Texas to the Troubles, launches in summer 2026, taking students, faculty and staff to Austin, Dublin and Belfast to study constructive dialogue and leadership in divided societies.

The vision behind all of it is simple to state: "We envision a peaceful and prosperous world for all." Getting there happens one partnership at a time.
