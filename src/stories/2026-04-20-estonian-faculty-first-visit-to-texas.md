In April, two faculty members from Tallinn University's social work program stood before an audience at The University of Texas at Austin. It was the first visit to Texas by Estonian faculty, and it marked a new phase in a partnership that began more than a decade ago.

Merike Sisask, whose research focuses on mental well-being and suicide prevention, and Karmen Toros, who studies child protection, presented at UT through the Erasmus+ exchange program. <!-- VERIFY: presentation titles, dates and host unit -->

## How it started

Diana DiNitto, a professor at the Steve Hicks School of Social Work, has taught in Tallinn University's social work doctoral program since 2014. Over six visits she has worked alongside Estonian colleagues and doctoral students, contributing to the training of social work researchers in a country where the field's academic footprint is still being built.

One result stands out. A dissertation DiNitto co-supervised won a national award from the Estonian Research Council, the first time the award had gone to a social work dissertation.

The partnership began, as DiNitto tells it, with an email.

> I am so glad I answered that email.
>
> — Diana DiNitto, Professor

## What the visit means

Faculty exchange is one of the quieter forms of global engagement. It does not involve cohorts of students or large grants. But it builds the relationships that everything else rests on: co-supervised dissertations, shared research questions and eventually the kind of two-way exchange that brought Sisask and Toros to Austin.

Their areas of research, suicide prevention and child protection, are urgent in Texas as well as in Estonia, and the questions they study cross borders easily. A visit like this one lets faculty on both sides compare approaches, test assumptions and find the places where their work overlaps.

For the Office of Global Engagement (OGE), the visit is a model of what reciprocal partnership looks like. For more than ten years, the exchange ran largely in one direction, with a UT professor traveling to Tallinn. Now it runs in both.

## Building the research pillar

Research is one of OGE's three pillars, alongside education abroad and service. The office's aim is to foster collaboration between universities, research institutions and organizations worldwide, and the Estonia partnership shows how that happens in practice: one faculty member says yes to an invitation, keeps showing up, and over a decade the relationship grows into something institutional.

Faculty interested in exploring exchange opportunities, in Estonia or elsewhere, can contact OGE.
