In 1993, the Steve Hicks School of Social Work placed a student with the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR) in Geneva. It was the first time the School had regularly placed students abroad, and it set a pattern. More than 30 years later, the UNHCR partnership is the School's longest-running international placement and one of its most distinctive.

## What interns do

The UNHCR placement is macro practice at global scale. Based at the agency's offices in Geneva, Switzerland, and Budapest, Hungary, interns work in program planning, monitoring and evaluation, and technical consulting: the work that determines how refugee responses are designed, measured and improved.

The crises they touch are among the world's most serious. Interns have contributed to UNHCR's refugee efforts in Ukraine, Syria, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Myanmar, Sudan and Haiti.

For a social work student, the placement offers something hard to find anywhere else: a view of how decisions made in a headquarters office reach a family in a camp thousands of miles away, and how the evidence gathered in that camp travels back to shape the next decision.

## How the placement works

The UNHCR internship is one of the School's global practicum options for final-year MSSW students, coordinated by the Office of Global Engagement (OGE) with the Office of Practicum Education and Texas Global. Students complete the semester-long placement in the spring, and Assistant Dean Stacey Jordan serves as faculty liaison.

Students who are interested should begin by contacting the liaison with questions about the agency and the application. ## Thirty years, and counting

Partnerships this long are rare in global education. They survive changes in leadership on both sides, shifts in funding and, in UNHCR's case, a world whose displacement crises have only multiplied. What has kept the placement going is that it works for both parties: the agency gains capable interns trained in evaluation and community practice, and the School's students gain an understanding of international systems they bring home to Texas.

That is the exchange OGE's tagline describes. Students leave to learn, and they return to lead.
