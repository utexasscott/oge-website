Kallathupatti is a village of about 3,000 residents in Tamil Nadu, in southern India. In 2021, a children's library opened there. Four years later, a team of UT Austin undergraduates arrived with a different kind of project: not to build something new, but to find out how well what had already been built was working.

The team was part of Projects with Underserved Communities (PUC), the yearlong service-learning sequence run jointly by Texas Global, the Cockrell School of Engineering and the Steve Hicks School of Social Work. Working with Church's Auxiliary for Social Action, the students evaluated the Kallathupatti library and a 2015 housing project for a nomadic community.

## Engineering and social work, side by side

PUC pairs undergraduate engineering and social work students for a full academic year. In the fall they take Project Development; in the spring, Project Design; and in May Term, Project Implementation, followed by travel the summer after. Along the way they practice project management, design, budgeting, fundraising, cross-cultural collaboration, community engagement and public speaking.

Since its launch in 2010, the program has sent teams to Mexico, Peru, Ghana, Costa Rica, Nicaragua, Guatemala, Tanzania, Papua New Guinea, India, Jamaica, Greece and Thailand, serving more than 18,300 people through projects that include water access, cookstoves, sanitation, accessibility, health clinics, solar power and community learning centers. <!-- VERIFY: launch year — Texas Global says 2010; The Utopian says 2009 -->

The India project took a reflective turn. Rather than deliver a structure, the team asked what a community needs from the organizations that come to serve it, and what those organizations owe the community after they leave.

## What the team produced

Farya Phillips of the School of Social Work led the India team. Its work resulted in three products: a set of community engagement protocols to guide future partnerships, a service-learning documentary and a book of community stories.

Phillips presented the project at the President's Award for Global Learning showcase in early November 2025. Her description of the program's philosophy is direct: students "learn from the communities they serve."

That orientation, with the community as teacher rather than beneficiary, is at the heart of how the School approaches global service. It is also why social work students are essential to a program that might otherwise read as purely technical. Evaluating a library or a housing project means listening carefully to the people who use them, and listening carefully is social work practice.
