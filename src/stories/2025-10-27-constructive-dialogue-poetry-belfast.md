What does poetry have to do with social work? For an evening in late October, the Steve Hicks School of Social Work made the case that the answer is: a great deal.

"Constructive Dialogue, Poetry and Social Imagination" brought four writers from the Seamus Heaney Centre at Queen's University Belfast to Austin: Glenn Patterson, Leontia Flynn, Dawn Watson and Rachel Brown. The event was connected to the Office of Global Engagement's growing work in Northern Ireland, and it was made possible with help from Robert Hull, Consul General of Ireland in Austin, and Jenny Browne, the 2018 Texas Poet Laureate and a 2020 Distinguished Fulbright Scholar, who helped bring the writers to campus.

## Why a school of social work

Northern Ireland lived through three decades of conflict, the Troubles, which claimed more than 3,500 lives before the 1998 Good Friday Agreement. In the years since, the work of reconciliation has been carried out not only in political negotiations but in the ordinary spaces where people tell their stories and listen to one another's.

Writers have a particular role in that work. Literature offers a way to hold more than one truth at once, a skill social workers need every day, whether in a clinic, a courtroom or a community meeting. The evening's title put the three ideas together: constructive dialogue, poetry and social imagination, the capacity to picture a society that does not yet exist and to work toward it.

The School's research interests in Northern Ireland follow the same thread. Faculty study reconciliation processes, how conflict and violence spread, and peacemaking and restorative justice. The visit put those themes in conversation with the writers' own practice.

## A step toward Belfast

The event was a precursor to From Texas to the Troubles, OGE's signature program launching in summer 2026. That course will take students, faculty and staff from Austin to Dublin and Belfast to study constructive dialogue and leadership in divided societies.

It also builds on an existing partnership. Through "Leading with Peace: Lessons from Northern Ireland," a President's Award for Global Learning program, the School's Noël Busch-Armendariz, Bruce Kellison and Monica Martinez have worked with 15 students from many majors and with Corrymeela, a community in Ballycastle whose mission is to build trust and understanding.

Bringing Belfast's writers to Austin reversed the usual direction of travel. Before Texans go to Northern Ireland to learn, Northern Ireland came to Texas.

## What comes next

OGE plans to keep building on its relationships with partners in Northern Ireland and with the Consulate General of Ireland as From Texas to the Troubles takes shape. Faculty interested in the Northern Ireland work, or in bringing international voices to campus, are encouraged to contact the office.
