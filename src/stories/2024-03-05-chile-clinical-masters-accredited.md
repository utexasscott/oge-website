When Chile's National Commission on Accreditation reviewed the clinical master's program in social work at Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile, one feature stood out: its collaboration with the Steve Hicks School of Social Work. The program has been accredited for 2023–2026, and the review cited the UT partnership as a key strength.

The program is the first clinical master's in social work in Chile, and it is the product of a long-standing alliance between the two schools. <!-- VERIFY: alliance length — described as roughly 11 years in 2026 sources -->

## A rotating faculty

Each semester, five School faculty members travel to Santiago. Each spends a week co-teaching an applied clinical course alongside Pontificia faculty, who lead the courses; the UT faculty's role is to strengthen clinical skills. The current pairings:

- **Biopsychosocial Clinical Evaluations:** Karla Gonzalez and Jesus Ortega
- **Parenting:** Olaya Grau and Rubén Parra-Cardona
- **Grief and Loss:** Valentina Garrido and Dede Sparks
- **Risk Behaviors and Diverse Youth:** Carolina Velasco and Rene Gaitan
- **Advanced Capstone:** Carolina Munoz and Donna Shanor

The model matters. Rather than importing a curriculum, the collaboration places Chilean faculty in the lead and brings UT faculty in as clinical partners, so the program stays rooted in Chile's own practice context while drawing on the School's depth in clinical education.

## By the numbers

Since the program began, 28 students have enrolled, with no dropouts, and the program admits about 17 students each year. <!-- VERIFY: enrollment and admissions figures are reported as of 2026 source material; confirm figures current to the story date -->

## A partner with deep roots

Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile's School of Social Work was founded in 1929 and is among Latin America's leading schools of social work. The collaboration with UT extends beyond the master's program to a Scholars in Residence arrangement, and faculty from both institutions have worked together on research. Rubén Parra-Cardona, the School's Associate Dean for Global Engagement, partners with Fundación San Carlos de Maipo on the cultural adaptation and large-scale dissemination of a parenting intervention that promotes social skills and prevents and reduces conduct problems in children.

"Global engagement represents one of the most strategic areas for our School," Parra-Cardona has said, and the Chile partnership is a case in point: a research collaboration, a teaching collaboration and a student placement, all with one partner.

Santiago is also one of the School's global practicum sites. MSSW students complete their final practicum there in a community-based hospital social work department or with the Network of Integral Accompaniment program, which serves refugees and people in need of international protection. Fluent Spanish is required for clinical placements, and Anita Guajardo serves as faculty liaison.

Faculty interested in joining a future teaching rotation can contact the Office of Global Engagement.
