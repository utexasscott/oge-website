The name means, roughly, "closing the gap."

The gap in question is in health and mental health: disparities in Puerto Rico that have been made worse by natural disasters and by the out-migration of the scholars who might otherwise be studying and addressing them. Cortando la Brecha, an initiative of the Steve Hicks School of Social Work, responds by building research capacity among early- and mid-career Puerto Rican scholars in the behavioral, social and health sciences.

## Listening first

The initiative began with three webinars. They were designed to share expertise, but they also served as a listening exercise, and what organizers heard was consistent. Scholars on the island did not lack ideas or ability. What they needed was protected time to write and access to technical assistance in developing fundable proposals.

So the next step was a writing retreat. In early fall 2023, participants gathered in Puerto Rico with senior researchers from UT Austin and from George Washington University's Gill-Lebovic Center for Community Health in the Caribbean and Latin America. The format was deliberately simple: time, space and experienced colleagues close at hand. <!-- VERIFY: retreat dates, location and number of participants -->

## A seminar for spring

The retreat leads into the next phase. In spring 2024, Rubén Parra-Cardona, the School's Associate Dean for Global Engagement, will lead a four-month grant-writing seminar for participating scholars, continuing the technical support the webinars identified as essential. The UT Latino Research Institute is a partner in the work.

> A global perspective makes us a humbler people and more accountable for our privileges.
>
> — Rubén Parra-Cardona, Associate Dean for Global Engagement

## Why it matters for the School

Cortando la Brecha illustrates how the research pillar of the School's new Office of Global Engagement (OGE) is meant to work: not as outside expertise parachuted in, but as a partnership that strengthens the people best placed to address a community's needs. The scholars who take part are the ones who will write the grants, run the studies and train the next generation on the island.

It is also part of a broader relationship with Puerto Rico. The School collaborates on research with Ponce Medical School as well. <!-- VERIFY: nature and status of the Ponce Medical School collaboration -->

OGE was created this year to lead, support and coordinate the School's global engagement initiatives, and Parra-Cardona serves as its founding Associate Dean. The office's three pillars are education abroad, research and service. Cortando la Brecha sits squarely in the second, but its purpose is the third: service to communities whose health depends on the research done in their name.

Faculty interested in Cortando la Brecha, or in similar capacity-building partnerships, can contact OGE.
