## Overview

Not every global experience needs a full semester. May Term courses pack a complete course into a few intensive weeks between the spring and summer sessions, so students can earn credit, work with agencies in another city and still finish the degree on time. The School of Social Work currently offers two: a long-running course in London and a new course in Washington, D.C.

Both courses are open to students who want to see how social welfare works somewhere else and bring that comparison home. "International social work has such a vital role to play in our world today," says Assistant Dean Tanya Voss, who co-leads the London course. "Now more than ever, it's vitally important that we educate our students…"<!-- VERIFY: the second quote is truncated in CONTENT-SOURCES.md; supply the full sentence before publishing. -->

## London: S W 360K / S W 495K

The London course examines the historical and contemporary contexts of the British welfare state, social work practice within it and the institutional efforts that have tried to address social injustice. Students look at community issues and social change from the ground up, working on projects with local social service agencies and living with vetted homestay families.

Why London? The British welfare state is one of the world's most studied, and it is still being debated and reshaped. Studying it alongside UK practitioners gives Texas students a live comparison for every policy debate they will join at home.

The course is led by Professors [Lauren Gulbas](https://socialwork.utexas.edu/directory/lauren-gulbas/) and [Tanya Voss](https://socialwork.utexas.edu/directory/tanya-voss/). Undergraduates enroll in S W 360K; graduate students enroll in S W 495K. Historically the course has run as a four-week Maymester preceded by four spring seminar sessions in Austin; current dates and format are published with the application.

## Washington, D.C.: Social Work Leadership for the Public Good

Social work's second home is the policy arena, and no city teaches it like Washington. Social Work Leadership for the Public Good is a new May Term course, open to graduate and undergraduate students, focused on policy advocacy and constituency building. Students learn how legislation and regulation are shaped, how coalitions are assembled and sustained and how social workers lead in public life.

The course is designed for students in every concentration who want to be effective beyond the clinic or agency, from community organizers to future administrators.

## A note on history: Ghana

May Term travel has been part of the School's signature education landscape for well over a decade. Around 2009, Dr. Dorie Gilbert led a Community and Social Development Maymester in Accra, Ghana, one of the earliest faculty-led short courses from the School. Ghana is no longer an active May Term site, but it remains part of the School's record of faculty-led programs abroad. See the [partner directory](/about/partners/) for past and present partnerships.

## Credit and cost

May Term courses carry regular UT course credit and appear on your transcript like any other course. According to Texas Global, full-time undergraduate students pay no additional tuition for May Term courses; program fees, travel and living costs are set separately and published with each program. Costs change from year to year, so rely on the current Texas Global program page rather than figures you may hear secondhand.

**For donors and partners:** short-term courses are often the first global experience a student can afford, and scholarships make the difference for many. Learn how to [support global engagement](/give/).
