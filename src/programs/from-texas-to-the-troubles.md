## Overview

For 30 years, Northern Ireland lived through the Troubles, a conflict that claimed more than 3,500 lives before the 1998 Good Friday Agreement brought it to a close. The peace that followed was not an accident. It was built, conversation by conversation, by people who learned to lead across lines that had seemed permanent.

From Texas to the Troubles is the Office of Global Engagement's signature program: a new international course, launched in summer 2026, in which students, faculty and staff study together in Austin, Dublin and Belfast.

Participants examine how conflict takes hold, what it costs and how communities move toward reconciliation, then bring those lessons home to a Texas that has its own divides to bridge.

The program grows out of the School's Northern Ireland reconciliation work, recognized with UT's President's Award for Global Learning, and out of a partnership with the Corrymeela Community, Northern Ireland's oldest peace and reconciliation organization.<!-- VERIFY: "oldest peace and reconciliation organization" is not in CONTENT-SOURCES.md --> As Associate Dean Noël Busch-Armendariz puts it, "Social workers address society's greatest challenges at home and abroad."

## Austin, Dublin and Belfast

<!-- VERIFY: itinerary framing below is drafted from the program description (Austin, Dublin, Belfast; constructive dialogue and leadership in divided societies). Confirm the role of each city, sequence and dates with the program faculty. -->

The course unfolds in three settings, each adding a layer to the whole.

**Austin.** The program begins on the Forty Acres with preparatory sessions that build a shared vocabulary: the history of the conflict, the frameworks of constructive dialogue and the habits of a cohort that will need to trust one another abroad. These sessions also connect the course to the University's broader conversation about leadership and civic life.

**Dublin.** In the Republic of Ireland, participants take in the island-wide context of the conflict and the peace process, including the role of the Irish state, civil society and culture in sustaining dialogue across the border.

**Belfast.** In Northern Ireland, the cohort meets the people and institutions that lived the Troubles and built the peace. Time with the Corrymeela Community and with writers and scholars connected to the Seamus Heaney Centre grounds the course in lived experience rather than textbook history.

Specific dates, durations and itinerary details for the next cohort will be published with the application. Join the [OGE listserv](/connect/listserv/) to be notified.

## What you'll learn

Participants leave the course able to:

- Explain how the Troubles began, spread and ended, and why the Good Friday Agreement has held.
- Practice constructive dialogue across deep political, religious and cultural difference.
- Recognize the social, economic and psychological costs of conflict and the mechanisms of reconciliation and restorative justice.
- Lead in divided settings, whether a community meeting in Texas, a hospital team or a policy debate.
- Apply a global perspective to local practice. In the words of the School's student-facing tagline: Leave to Learn. Return to Lead.

The course is interdisciplinary by design. Leading with Peace drew 15 students from many majors, and From Texas to the Troubles carries that model forward, pairing social work students with peers from across the University.

## Who should apply

From Texas to the Troubles is open to students, faculty and staff. You do not need a background in Irish history or in conflict studies. What matters is a willingness to listen, to sit with discomfort and to lead with humility. Students in social work, public affairs, history, business, education, communication and the health professions will all find direct applications.

Eligibility requirements, costs and funding options will be posted with the application. Priority deadline: see the current application.

## Our partners

**Corrymeela Community, Ballycastle.** Founded to "build trust and understanding," Corrymeela is Northern Ireland's oldest peace and reconciliation organization and the School's partner on both Leading with Peace and From Texas to the Troubles.

**Seamus Heaney Centre, Queen's University Belfast.** In late October 2025, the Centre's writers and artists, including Glenn Patterson, Leontia Flynn, Dawn Watson and Rachel Brown, joined the School in Austin for "Constructive Dialogue, Poetry and Social Imagination," a public precursor event exploring the role of the arts in divided societies. Robert Hull, Consul General of Ireland in Austin, and poet Jenny Browne, 2018 Texas Poet Laureate and 2020 Distinguished Fulbright Scholar, helped bring the event to campus.

## Related research

From Texas to the Troubles is the teaching arm of a research agenda. "Leading with Peace: Lessons from Northern Ireland," led by Noël Busch-Armendariz with Bruce Kellison and Monica Martinez, examines reconciliation processes, how conflict and violence spread, and peacemaking and restorative justice. Read more on the [Northern Ireland research area page](/research/northern-ireland/) or browse the [faculty research directory](/research/faculty/).
