## Overview

Over 30 years, until the 1998 Good Friday Agreement, the Troubles claimed more than 3,500 lives in Northern Ireland. The peace that followed is one of the most studied in the world, and still one of the most instructive: a divided society that chose dialogue over violence and has had to keep choosing it, year after year.

The Office of Global Engagement's Northern Ireland research area studies that choice. Working with the Corrymeela Community, Northern Ireland's oldest peace and reconciliation organization,<!-- VERIFY: "oldest peace and reconciliation organization" is not in CONTENT-SOURCES.md --> UT faculty and students examine how conflict begins and spreads, what it costs, and how reconciliation and restorative justice take root. The work is explicitly comparative: what Northern Ireland learned is meant to inform practice and leadership in Texas and beyond.

## Leading with Peace: Lessons from Northern Ireland

The flagship project, "Leading with Peace: Lessons from Northern Ireland," was honored with The University of Texas at Austin's President's Award for Global Learning, which recognizes interdisciplinary teams of faculty and students working on a global challenge with a partner abroad.<!-- VERIFY: description of the award's criteria is drafted; confirm against the President's Award for Global Learning site. -->

Leading with Peace brought together 15 students from many majors under three faculty leads, with Corrymeela in Ballycastle as the community partner. Corrymeela's mission is to "build trust and understanding," and UT students worked alongside Corrymeela staff and the communities they serve, studying reconciliation not as an abstraction but as a daily practice.

## Research questions

The program is organized around three themes that run through the School's broader work on violence and its aftermath:

- **Reconciliation processes.** What institutional, community and interpersonal mechanisms allow former adversaries to live and govern together? Which hold up over decades, and which erode?
- **How conflict and violence spread.** How does political violence travel through families, neighborhoods and economies, and what interrupts the transmission?
- **Peacemaking and restorative justice.** What can social work, economics and history learn from Northern Ireland's restorative practices, and how do those lessons translate to communities in the United States?

Each theme carries direct implications for social work practice: in domestic violence and sexual assault response, in work with communities marked by historical violence and in the training of leaders who must act across difference.

## Partner: Corrymeela Community

Corrymeela, based in Ballycastle, is Northern Ireland's oldest peace and reconciliation organization, the School's primary partner in Northern Ireland and the anchor for both the research program and its teaching counterpart. The relationship now also includes a second Northern Ireland partner, the Seamus Heaney Centre at Queen's University Belfast, whose writers and artists joined the School in Austin in October 2025 for "Constructive Dialogue, Poetry and Social Imagination." Browse the [partner directory](/about/partners/).

## From research to the classroom

The research program has a direct teaching counterpart. [From Texas to the Troubles](/education/from-texas-to-the-troubles/), OGE's signature program launched in summer 2026, takes students, faculty and staff from Austin to Dublin and Belfast to study constructive dialogue and leadership in divided societies. It was built on the findings and relationships of Leading with Peace, and the two continue to feed each other: students in the course contribute observations and questions that shape the next phase of research.
