## Overview

Many students at the Steve Hicks School of Social Work already build a global thread through their degree: an International Social Work elective here, a May Term in London there, a final practicum in Cape Town or Oaxaca, and a seminar that ties it together. The Global Social Work Certificate is a proposed pathway to recognize that thread with a credential, so the work shows up on a résumé and in a licensing or doctoral application as the coherent specialization it is.

The pathway is being developed by the Office of Global Engagement with the School's academic leadership. It is intended to be portfolio-style: built from courses and experiences the School already offers rather than a separate program with its own admissions. Students would declare the pathway, complete the components over the course of their degree and submit a capstone reflection at the end.

## Proposed components

<!-- VERIFY: components, sequencing and credit counts -->

The current proposal has four parts.

<ol class="steps">
  <li><h3>International Social Work elective</h3><p>A foundation course in comparative social welfare, global social problems and the ethics of cross-border practice.</p></li>
  <li><h3>Global Practicum Seminar</h3><p>The seminar that prepares students for cross-cultural practice and sustains the cohort while they are abroad, taught by Assistant Dean Tanya Voss.</p></li>
  <li><h3>A global experience</h3><p>Either a <a href="/education/practicum/">Global Practicum</a> (the final MSSW practicum completed abroad) or a <a href="/education/may-term/">May Term course</a> in London or Washington, D.C. Participation in <a href="/education/from-texas-to-the-troubles/">From Texas to the Troubles</a> or <a href="/education/projects-with-underserved-communities/">Projects with Underserved Communities</a> may be considered.</p></li>
  <li><h3>Capstone reflection</h3><p>A written or multimedia portfolio connecting the global experience to the student's practice at home, reviewed by OGE faculty.</p></li>
</ol>

## Who it is for

<!-- VERIFY: eligible degree levels -->

The pathway is proposed first for MSSW students, whose practicum structure and elective space fit the components most naturally. An undergraduate option built around May Term courses and Projects with Underserved Communities is under consideration. International students enrolled at the School would be eligible on the same terms as domestic students.

## Why a certificate

Employers in international organizations, refugee resettlement, global health and policy increasingly look for evidence of cross-cultural competence. A certificate makes that evidence legible. It also gives students a reason to plan: choosing the elective early, sequencing the seminar and the practicum and reflecting on the whole at the end rather than treating each piece as a one-off.

For the School, the pathway is a way to carry forward Dean Allan Cole's observation that "global engagement has been a part of our signature education landscape for more than 20 years," and to make that landscape navigable for the next generation of students.

**For donors and partners:** a named certificate creates natural opportunities for scholarship support tied to global experiences, and for partner agencies to host students who arrive with structured preparation. Learn how to [support global engagement](/give/).

## What happens next

The proposal must move through the School's curriculum and approval processes before students can declare it. No application is open, and no requirements are final. If you are a current student who wants to position yourself for the pathway, the best preparation is to take the International Social Work elective when it is offered, talk with your advisor about a global practicum or May Term, and [join the OGE listserv](/connect/listserv/) for announcements.

Questions about the proposal can go to [Tanya Voss](https://socialwork.utexas.edu/directory/tanya-voss/), Assistant Dean for Global Engagement.
