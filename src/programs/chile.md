## Overview

The School's relationship with Chile is among the deepest of its research partnerships. For about 11 years, faculty from the Steve Hicks School of Social Work have worked with the Escuela de Trabajo Social at Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile, founded in 1929 and among the leading schools of social work in Latin America. Out of that alliance came something neither institution could have built alone: Chile's first clinical master's degree in social work.

The partnership now runs on three tracks. UT faculty co-teach in the clinical master's program each semester. Scholars move between Austin and Santiago as scholars in residence. And a parallel research program with Fundación San Carlos de Maipo is adapting and scaling an evidence-based parenting intervention for Chilean families. MSSW students join the picture through the [Santiago global practicum](/education/practicum/#santiago).

## Clinical Master's Program Collaboration

Until this program, Chile had no master's degree dedicated to clinical social work. Pontificia faculty lead the program, and the School's role is to strengthen its clinical core. Each semester, five UT faculty rotate through Santiago, each spending a week co-teaching an applied clinical course. Pontificia faculty lead the courses; UT faculty bring clinical depth and current U.S. practice models.

### Accreditation and results

The program was accredited by Chile's National Commission on Accreditation for 2023–2026, and the review cited the collaboration with the School as a key strength. To date, 28 students have enrolled, with a dropout rate of zero and roughly 17 admissions each year. Students, the program's director Liliana Guerra and program chief M. Olaya Grau have all spoken about the collaboration's value; their testimonials will be added as they are cleared for publication.

## Scholars in Residence

Alongside the teaching rotation, the alliance supports scholars in residence: extended visits in which faculty from one institution spend time at the other to teach, consult on curriculum and develop joint research. The residencies are what turn a teaching exchange into a research partnership, giving collaborators the sustained time that grant proposals and co-authored work require.

## Parenting research with Fundación San Carlos de Maipo

Professor [Rubén Parra-Cardona](https://socialwork.utexas.edu/directory/ruben-parra-cardona/), the School's founding Associate Dean for Global Engagement, leads a collaboration with Fundación San Carlos de Maipo on the cultural adaptation and large-scale dissemination of a parenting intervention. The program promotes children's social skills and helps prevent, reduce and reverse moderate to severe conduct problems.

The work matters for two reasons. First, it brings a tested prevention program to Chilean families at scale, through a foundation with the reach to sustain it. Second, it is a model of how cultural adaptation should be done: with local partners shaping the intervention rather than receiving it. As Parra-Cardona has said, "A global perspective makes us a humbler people and more accountable for our privileges."

## Looking ahead

Dean Allan Cole visited Chile in 2026. He noted that future work could align with the School's Center for Societal Impact, connecting the Chilean collaboration to the School's broader agenda on community-engaged research. "Global engagement represents one of the most strategic areas for our School," Parra-Cardona has observed, and Chile is where that strategy is most visible.
