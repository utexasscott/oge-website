## Overview

The Texas Initiative Enhancing Rural-Border Solutions, or TIES, is a Steve Hicks School of Social Work initiative focused on rural and border communities in Texas, where the local and the global are the same thing. Families along the Texas–Mexico border live binational lives. Rural counties face the same shortages of behavioral health care, housing and transportation that social workers encounter around the world, with fewer resources to meet them.

TIES was recognized by the Moritz Center for Societal Impact as a Projects for Societal Impact awardee. Its team brings together Noël Busch-Armendariz, Monica Martinez and Gloria González-López, an interdisciplinary group whose work spans gender-based violence, border history and community well-being.<!-- VERIFY: team members' roles and departmental affiliations on TIES -->

The Office of Global Engagement lists TIES under both Research and Service because it is both: a program of community-engaged inquiry and a commitment to being useful to the communities it studies.

## Why a border initiative belongs in global engagement

It is easy to think of global engagement as something that happens on a plane. OGE's view is broader. The same skills that students build in Oaxaca or Cape Town, including language, humility, cross-cultural assessment and partnership with local institutions, are exactly the skills that rural and border Texas needs. And the knowledge that comes back from the border, about migration, binational families, trauma and resilience, informs the School's work abroad.

TIES is the place where those two directions meet. It connects faculty who work internationally with communities a few hours from campus, and it gives students a way to practice global social work without leaving the state.

## Focus areas

<!-- VERIFY: focus areas are proposed, drawn from the team's documented expertise; replace with the initiative's actual aims -->

The initiative's proposed focus areas reflect the team's expertise:

- **Violence, safety and justice.** Building on the Institute on Domestic Violence & Sexual Assault's research, understanding how gender-based violence and human trafficking affect rural and border communities and how local systems respond.
- **Historical memory and community trust.** Drawing on Monica Martinez's scholarship on border violence and public history, examining how histories of violence shape present-day trust in institutions, including social services.
- **Binational families and well-being.** Studying the everyday realities of families whose lives cross the border and what culturally grounded practice looks like for them.
- **Rural solutions.** Identifying and strengthening the community-based approaches that already work in rural Texas.

## Connections across OGE

TIES shares faculty and questions with the School's [Northern Ireland research area](/research/northern-ireland/), where Busch-Armendariz and Martinez study how violence spreads and how communities reconcile. The comparison between a post-conflict society in Europe and the Texas–Mexico borderlands is deliberate: both are places where history, identity and institutional trust shape what help people will accept.

The initiative also complements the School's faculty-led work in Mexico, including a visiting scholar relationship with Universidad Autónoma de Baja California and collaboration with UNAM. See the [partner directory](/about/partners/) for those relationships.
